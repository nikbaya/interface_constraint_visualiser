Methods

Data

Per-residue constraint: gnomAD v4.1 per-residue missense observed and expected counts (summed over all possible missense changes at a residue), keyed to the UniProt canonical sequence. Interfaces: Genomics2Proteins (G2P) inter-chain contacts from PDB structures — hydrogen bonds, non-bonded interactions, salt bridges and disulfides. A residue is an interface residue if it makes any inter-chain contact in any PDB entry. Proteins are included when every residue's reference amino acid agrees between gnomAD and G2P.

Interface vs non-interface

O/E = observed / expected missense, pooled over interface or non-interface residues. Intervals are two-sided 95% gamma (Poisson) intervals on the observed count. The depletion test is a one-sided conditional binomial (C-test, Przyborowski & Wilenski 1940): given the total observed count, is the interface's share smaller than its share of expected? Significant means p < 0.05, uncorrected. When O/E = 0 (no observed missense) the interval is unreliable at the boundary and is flagged.

Partner-resolved constraint

Each G2P contact names the partner chain in a PDB entry; the partner protein is identified because that chain appears as the own chain in another protein's G2P file (G2P-only mapping; chains claimed by no G2P protein — antibodies, non-human proteins, peptides — are pooled as unidentified chain). Interfaces seen in several entries are de-duplicated into binding modes (overlap coefficient ≥ 0.6, average linkage). For partner B, this protein's interface with B is the union of its residues contacting B in any entry; its O/E is compared with the rest of the protein by the same one-sided conditional binomial. Partner heterogeneity is a Poisson likelihood-ratio G-test of a common O/E across partners, on partner-exclusive residues only (so counts are independent), partners with expected ≥ 2, proteins with ≥ 2 such partners; q is Benjamini–Hochberg across tested proteins. Homomeric contacts (another copy of the same protein) are shown as a homomer row; contacts between two processed chains of one precursor are kept apart.

ClinVar

ClinVar (2026-04, GRCh38) single amino-acid substitutions, mapped by gene symbol and HGVS protein change. Residue-level: a residue counts once per category (P/LP, VUS, B/LB). Enrichment at the interface is a one-sided binomial of the P/LP interface share against the protein's interface fraction.

Disease genes

OMIM disease gene: the gene has at least one OMIM morbidmap phenotype with mapping key 3 (molecular basis known), excluding non-disease […], susceptibility {…} and provisional ? entries (morbidmap 2026-02-13). Only this flag and a link to the OMIM entry are shown. ClinVar P/LP: at least one pathogenic / likely-pathogenic missense substitution in ClinVar (see above).

Structures

For each partner, the representative entry is the binding mode with most PDB support, taking the instance with most contact residues. Coordinates are fetched from the RCSB ModelServer (chain subsets), falling back to PDBe. UniProt↔PDB numbering uses SIFTS segments stored at export; chains without a stored mapping assume UniProt numbering (noted in the panel).

Reproducibility & caveats

All numbers on this site are aggregated, derived values precomputed from the analysis repository; raw gnomAD and structural data never ship. Interfaces are limited to proteins and partners with solved structures, so absence of a partner means no structure, not no interaction. Expected counts are gnomAD's mutational-model expectations; residues with no expected count are shown in grey.
